# SRS structure

Organize a Software Requirements Specification with these sections:

1. Introduction — purpose, intent, boundaries, and background
2. Scope — terminology, in scope, out of scope, assumptions and external dependencies, actors, and interactions or use cases
3. Functional Requirements — grouped by functional areas that come from the TOR, with stable identifiers and statements
4. Nonfunctional Requirements — only categories the project justifies
5. Acceptance Criteria — verifiable, and linked to requirements
6. Constraints and References — technical and organizational constraints, plus traceable references

## Rules

- Derive requirement groups from the project TOR. Do not reuse another project's grouping labels.
- Include a diagram only when the TOR or an approved elaboration supports it. Do not invent one.
- Do not add every nonfunctional category by default.
- Do not invent numerical thresholds, capacities, or timings.
- State unresolved items visibly. Do not hide them inside a requirement.
- Do not prescribe architecture, technology, or deployment unless the frozen TOR or an approved project decision requires it.
